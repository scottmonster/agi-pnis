# High Compression

Use high compression to produce the smallest artifact that satisfies the selected operation and all required rules.

Treat reduced size as a primary objective.
The selected operation defines the authorized boundary, required material, and preservation obligations.
High compression changes representation within that boundary. It does not change what is authorized, required, or preserved.
Preserve every required semantic, functional, operational, access, compatibility, authority, and verification requirement.
Within the authorized boundary, wording, order, headings, repetition, examples, and formatting may change as far as the selected operation permits.

## Method

1. Follow the selected operation and required rules to determine the material and constraints within the authorized boundary.
2. Express each in-scope requirement, action, condition, exception, dependency, decision, warning, or verification criterion in its smallest usable form.
3. Rebuild the authorized material when a new structure is more compact and remains correct for the intended consumer.
4. Combine, generalize, relocate, or remove content only when the resulting artifact still satisfies every applicable requirement.
5. Verify that the result remains within scope and satisfies the selected operation and required rules.

## Compression Techniques

- **Protect material first:** Identify what the selected operation requires. Compress everything else as far as possible.
- **Delete before rewriting:** Remove redundancy, repetition, examples, explanation, and structure that do not materially support the artifact.
- **Merge overlapping material:** Combine statements only when their meaning, force, scope, conditions, exceptions, dependencies, and outcomes remain intact.
- **Use denser representations:** Change the structure, not just the wording. Use tables, schemas, compact rules, or other shorter forms when they preserve required access, usability, and format conformance.
- **Rebuild where permitted:** Do not preserve existing wording, order, headings, or formatting merely because they exist. Rebuild only to the extent the selected operation authorizes.
- **Generalize safely:** Replace repeated cases with a general rule only when the individual cases have no separate material meaning.
- **Challenge every element:** If removing an element would not materially affect the authorized result, remove it.
- **Choose the smallest valid form:** When several representations satisfy the selected operation and required rules, use the one with the least text and structure.
- **Use no more tokens than necessary:** Prefer the shortest expressions that satisfy scope requirements.

## Stop Condition

Stop when further reduction would impair required meaning, correct use, access, compatibility, verification, or format conformance.
