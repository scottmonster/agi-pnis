* Clarity - makes behavior and intent easier to understand.
* Maintainability - makes future changes safer and easier.
* Cognitive load - reduces how much state, nesting, or context a developer must hold mentally.
* Debuggability - makes faults easier to locate and reason about.
* Change safety - reduces the chance that modifying one thing unintentionally breaks another.
* Testability - makes behavior easier to isolate and verify.
* Predictability - conventional structures behave in ways experienced developers recognize quickly.
* Reviewability - makes mistakes, unusual logic, and suspicious structures easier to spot during review.
* Local reasoning - allows a developer to understand a piece of code without tracing large portions of the system.
* Reduced coupling - prevents unrelated parts of the system from depending too heavily on each other.
* Reduced duplication - prevents multiple copies of logic from drifting apart.
* Correctness - simpler, clearer control and data flow generally provides fewer opportunities for defects.
* Evolvability - makes it easier to extend or replace behavior as requirements change.
